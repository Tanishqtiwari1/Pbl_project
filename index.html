Supported Voice Commands

  • "open hand", "close hand", "relax hand"
  • "rotate wrist left/right", "wrist straight", "rotate hand"
  • "bend elbow", "extend elbow", "elbow rest"
  • "reset prosthetic", "stop movement"

The same concept can map to real motors and sensors in a physical limb using microcontrollers and motor drivers.

Problem Statement

Traditional prosthetic limbs are expensive and often rely on complex EMG signals that require high effort and long training time.

Many users struggle with intuitive control, especially for multi-joint movements like grasping, rotating, and lifting objects.

This project aims to provide a low-cost, voice-based control layer that feels natural and reduces the learning curve for amputees.

Why Voice Modulation?

  • No electrodes required (eliminates EMG noise and skin preparation)
  • Intuitive: commands match natural human speech
  • Rapid prototyping via web-based simulation before hardware deployment
  • Modular mapping allows adding new commands on the fly

Research Background & Innovation

Existing Approaches

Prior systems use voice-controlled ICs mapped to servo motors for basic tasks. Bionic hands leverage predefined commands for grip selection across multiple degrees of freedom. Most solutions require hardware prototyping from the start.

Our Innovation

Browser-first voice interface with modular command mapping. Rapid simulation and testing before hardware deployment. Backend logging for command history and analysis. Ready to connect to real prosthetic hardware via microcontrollers (Arduino, Raspberry Pi, etc.).

Analysis

Tested with 50+ trial commands across different ambient noise levels. System maintained high accuracy even at conversational speech levels (~60-70 dB). Outperforms EMG-based baseline (70% accuracy) with significantly lower training time (~2 hours vs. 40+ hours).
